Integration

One call where you approve videos today.

Recruiting, tasks and payouts stay in your app. Ormes returns the decision by webhook or by polling.

1

Write the rubric once

From your lab’s spec, one per task. Every decision records the version it used.

2

Send each upload

videos.upload() in TypeScript or Python, or three REST calls.

3

Act on the decision

Pay, reject with the reason, or route to your reviewers. Signed webhooks, typed errors, retries built in.

import { Ormes } from "@ormes/sdk";

const qx = new Ormes(); // reads ORMES_API_KEY

const video = await qx.videos.upload("./v_88214.mp4", {
  rubricId: "rub_load_dishwasher",
  contributorRef: "c_4086",
});
const d = await qx.videos.waitForDecision(video.id);

d.decision;      // "reject"
d.reasons[0];    // hands_visible.below_min, 61% (min 80%)

What Ormes doesn’t do.

It doesn’t sell your data.

We check your videos and delete them. We don’t offer them to labs or to other customers.

It doesn’t take the last word.

You set the rubric, reviewers can override any decision, and contributors can contest a rejection with a person.

It doesn’t promise what your lab will accept.

It applies the spec you give it, the same way on every video. Send your lab’s decisions and we measure ourselves against them.

It doesn’t reject when it isn’t sure.

A doubtful case goes to review. A wrong rejection costs a contributor money, so we treat it as the worst mistake.

Questions

We already check uploads. Why Ormes?

Upload checks catch technical problems. The expensive calls are hands, steps and privacy, which today need a person. Ormes makes those calls with reasons and sends only the doubtful ones to your reviewers.

Why not prompt a general video model?

General models are cheap, but in published tests of judging whether a task succeeded the best one is wrong about one time in four, and they lean toward “success”. Those are the videos your lab sends back. Ormes uses its own models for hands and tasks, and sends doubtful cases to review.

Who sets the criteria?

You, from your lab’s spec. Each task has its own rubric, and every decision records the rubric version it used.

How accurate is it?

We report precision and recall on rejections against your own reject rate, on videos your reviewers or your lab have already judged. That is the pilot report.

Does Ormes keep my videos?

No. Videos are deleted after analysis; those waiting for review within 14 days. We keep decisions, reasons and measurements so you have a record of every call.
