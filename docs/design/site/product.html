The decisionChecksRubricsReviewContestsLab outcomes

Three answers, one rule.

Every check produces measurements. The rubric turns them into reasons. The reasons decide.

reject

At least one reason has a reject effect: a critical check failed with a clear measurement, such as hands visible 61% against a minimum of 80%.

review

Something needs a person: a non-critical check failed, a face was found, or a model was not confident enough. Never a silent reject.

accept

No reasons at all. Every enabled check passed. The decision still carries every measurement and the rubric version.

Each check can be critical (a failure rejects) or not (a failure goes to review), or turned off.

A person can always contest.

A contributor who disagrees with a rejection opens a contest from a link. They see only the reasons that caused the rejection; a person on your team decides.

Only what matters

Contributors see the reasons that rejected the video, never internal signals such as duplicate matching.

Decided by a person

Upheld or overturned, with a note. The outcome is stored with the video.

Measured

Overturned rejections count as our mistakes in your precision report.

Measured against your lab, not against itself.

Send the lab’s accept or reject for delivered videos. Ormes reports precision and recall on rejections against your reject rate, per rubric version, and proposes threshold changes as a new version you approve.
